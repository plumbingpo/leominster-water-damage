Kitchen Water Damage Help in Leominster

No room has as many water connections as the kitchen, with its faucet, drain, disposal, dishwasher and refrigerator line. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. A drip from any of them travels to the rear of the cabinet base, then spreads beneath the flooring. Our Leominster crew locates the leak, dries what it wet, and keeps as much of your kitchen intact as we can.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

Signs to Look For

  • Swollen or darkened toe kicks, or ones separating from the base of the cabinets
  • Cupped or raised floorboards, wood or laminate, near the dishwasher door
  • Swelling and flaking where the cabinet sides meet the floor
  • A water bill that climbs, or a faint hiss or drip sound, with every tap turned off
  • The ceiling under the kitchen has developed a brown ring
  • Warping, staining or a soft spot in the floor of the cabinet below the sink
How We Fix It

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Shut Off the Leak

We shut off the leaking fixture or appliance line, pull the dishwasher or refrigerator if needed, and confirm exactly which connection failed so it can be repaired before drying begins.

2

Measure the Wet Area

We check the cabinet bases, the wall at their back and the floor throughout the room with thermal imaging and moisture meters. It is common for water beneath a kitchen floor to spread further than you would guess.

3

Dry in Place

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. An antimicrobial is applied to damp cavities while air movers and dehumidifiers work over several days.

4

Remove Only Damage

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. After dry readings are confirmed, the appliances go back in and we help you arrange the remaining repairs.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Under the Base Cabinets

Every side of the space beneath base cabinets is closed off. Water pools in it for weeks, and the mold that grows goes unseen until the cabinets come out.

Behind the Dishwasher

Slowly dripping supply or drain fittings soak the cabinet sides beside the unit and the subfloor below. None of it is visible behind the appliance until the flooring starts to change.

Drips at the Sink Drain

Disposal seals and drain fittings that are loose leak only when water is going down, which makes them easy to overlook. A damp sink cabinet smells and grows mold quickly because that water has food waste in it.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

The wall at the back of the sink and dishwasher holds drain and supply pipes. A leak there wets drywall and framing that the cabinets cover completely, so mold can spread across the wall unseen.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

First hour

Water Runs Under

Water from a failed hose or fitting flows to the back of the cabinets and under the appliances. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

The water is absorbed by particleboard bases and toe kicks, which swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Days 3 through 7

Flooring Starts to Move

Laminate swells at the edges, hardwood cups, and vinyl lifts once the adhesive under it fails. Drawers and cabinet doors stop lining up.

After a week

Tear Out Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. The subfloor may be coming apart in layers, and mold behind cabinets often means they come out together with the countertop.
